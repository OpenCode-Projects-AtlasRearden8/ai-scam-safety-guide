# Glossary

Plain-English definitions, alphabetical. When a term has a fuller
discussion, the chapter is noted.

**AI-written scam** — A scam message produced by an AI writing tool, so it
arrives with polished grammar. Since broken grammar used to be the main
warning sign, these scams require context checks instead:
[chapter 02](02-spotting-ai-written-scams.md).

**Business email compromise (BEC)** — A scam aimed at businesses and
families: a message that reads exactly like a boss, landlord, or accountant,
asking you to send a real payment to a "new" account. Killed by the
invoice-change rule: [chapter 07](07-verifying-people-and-payments.md).

**Chargeback** — A reversal of a card payment you can request through your
bank when something was fraud. Works fairly well for card purchases; not at
all for gift cards, wires, or crypto.

**Chatbot** — A program you talk with, primarily in text. The real, well-known
ones are valuable; their *imitators* collect logins and card numbers:
[chapter 05](05-fake-ai-tool-sites.md).

**Clone (voice clone)** — An AI copy of a person's voice, buildable from a
few seconds of recorded audio, good enough to fool the people who love
them. The defense is the family passphrase and the call-back drill:
[chapter 04](04-cloned-voice-calls.md).

**Credential stuffing** — Using passwords leaked from one site to break into
accounts on other sites where the same person reused them. This is why
a password used on a fake AI tool site is worth real money:
[chapter 05](05-fake-ai-tool-sites.md).

**Deepfake** — Any AI-generated or AI-edited photo, video, or voice of
something that never happened. Photos: [chapter 03](03-deepfake-photos.md);
voices: [chapter 04](04-cloned-voice-calls.md).

**Deposit scam** — Being asked for a small "holding" payment to reserve a
too-good item; deposits to unknown strangers are usually the whole scam:
[chapter 06](06-too-good-deals.md).

**Escalation (channel move)** — The scammer's habit of pulling the
conversation to a "quieter" place: "let's text instead," "email me
directly," "don't discuss this with the store." A way to leave behind the
rules of wherever the scam started.

**Family passphrase** — A code word shared among close people, asked for
during any emergency call that involves money or secrecy. Not names, dates or
pets — random and boring on purpose: [chapter 04](04-cloned-voice-calls.md),
with the setup conversation in [chapter 09](09-family-friday-talk.md).

**Gift card scam** — A payment demand in gift cards ("Apple/Google cards
from the register"). Gift cards are effectively cash without a trail;
no legitimate organization collects money this way.

**Invoice-change scam** — The practical heart of BEC: a "new bank account"
for a payment you were already planning. Verified by phone call to a number
you already had, every time.

**Overpayment scam** — A stranger "accidentally" overpays you and asks you to
refund the difference; their original payment then evaporates and your
refund was real money out the door. See the "pay-to-receive" rule below.

**Pay-to-receive scam** — Any request to pay money in order to receive
money (grants, prizes, refunds, "processing fees"). Honest money flows one
direction: [chapter 07](07-verifying-people-and-payments.md).

**Phishing** — A fake message designed to make you click a link or give up
credentials. The general how-it-works and what links look like are covered in
*The Privacy Comfort Handbook* (its chapter 04); this guide sticks to the
AI-dressed versions: [chapter 02](02-spotting-ai-written-scams.md).

**Pig-butchering scam** — The practice name for the long-warm kindness
scam: a friendly stranger (with a too-perfect photo) spends weeks building
trust, then introduces an "investment opportunity" that eats your savings.
Seen in story 5: [stories](stories.md).

**Reverse image search** — Searching with an image instead of words; tells
you where else that photo has appeared. The fastest way to catch a fake
profile photo or a cabin that isn't theirs: [chapter 03](03-deepfake-photos.md).

**Recovery scam** — A second scam targeting recent scam victims, promising
to recover their lost money for a fee. Rule: anyone who contacts *you*
promising recovery is the same crime, twice:
[chapter 08](08-what-to-do-if-you-fell-for-one.md).

**Sextortion** — A scam where intimate photos or messages are threatened
into leverage. Response: no payments, preserve messages, block, report,
tell a trusted person immediately: [chapter 08](08-what-to-do-if-you-fell-for-one.md).

**Smishing** — Phishing that arrives over SMS text messages.

**Spoofing** — Making contact information look like it belongs to someone
else: caller-ID can display your bank's real number; email display names
can say anything. This is why the trusted-channel rule (call the number
you already have) beats "check who's calling."

**Synthetic media** — The umbrella term for content made or altered by AI —
deepfakes, cloned voices, generated images. This guide's shorthand: "made
in a factory."

**Two-factor login (2FA)** — A second lock on an account, usually a
correct-once code. Covered properly in *The Privacy Comfort Handbook*
(chapter 03); appears here in the recovery checklist in
[chapter 08](08-what-to-do-if-you-fell-for-one.md).

**Typosquatting** — Registering look-alike domains ("gptfree4you.io," one
letter off a real brand) to catch skimmers. Defeated by reading domains
out loud: [chapter 05](05-fake-ai-tool-sites.md) and
[chapter 06](06-too-good-deals.md).

**Verification code** — The one-time code a service texts or emails you.
Reading it to anyone, for any reason, hands them your account.
Never-shared rule appears throughout, e.g.
[chapter 06](06-too-good-deals.md).

**Verification code screen trick** — The marketplace scam variant: "read
me the code to prove *you're* real." The code proves *them* — into your
account. Same defense: codes are never read aloud.

**Verification ladder** — The three-rung habit for checking who you're
talking to: restate on the channel you chose → ask for shared knowledge →
confirm through a third source unrelated to the requester:
[chapter 07](07-verifying-people-and-payments.md).

**Vishing** — Phishing by voice: a phone call playing a role (bank fraud
line, court clerk, tax agent). Today it may also carry a cloned voice:
[chapter 04](04-cloned-voice-calls.md).

**Wire transfer** — A direct bank-to-bank payment with essentially no
reversal. Legitimate institutions ask you to wire; scammers love it. When a
stranger requests one, the payment traffic light turns red:
[chapter 07](07-verifying-people-and-payments.md).
