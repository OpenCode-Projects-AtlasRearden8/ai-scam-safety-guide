# 04: Cloned-Voice Calls

This is the chapter to read twice.

Voice cloning used to need studio recordings and hours of setup. Today,
a scammer can build a passable copy of a voice from **a few seconds of
audio** — a voicemail greeting, a clip from a public video, even the voice
note a child put on social media. Then they call their target, and the
voice on the phone is someone you love, in trouble, asking you to help.

If you're a grandparent, a parent, or anyone whose loved ones trust their
ears, this chapter is your fire drill.

## What the call usually sounds like

- **A crisis fast.** An accident, an arrest, a hospital, a missed flight
  abroad — and a need for money *now* (bail, a lawyer's deposit, a taxi, a
  hotel).
- **An excuse why you can't check.** "My phone died." "I'm using a borrowed
  phone." "Please don't tell Mom, she'll panic." Each excuse exists to break
  your one reflex that could save you: calling them on the number you know.
- **A stranger "confirming."** A "lawyer," "doctor," or "officer" joins,
  tells you the payment method (gift cards, wire, crypto), and applies
  pressure exactly when you try to think.
- **Crying or crackling audio.** Bad connections are normal; streamed
  cloned audio sometimes sounds slightly compressed. But do **not** rely on
  your ear — that's the whole point of this chapter. Some clones are
  indistinguishable.

## Why "it sounded exactly like them" fails

Because the thing that made it sound like them — the voice — is now the
thing that's easiest to fake. The one thing that *still* can't be copied is
shared knowledge between two real people. That's your protection, and it's
free.

## The family passphrase

Set up a code word with your closest people (chapter 09 has the
conversation script for starting this). Rules that make it work:

- **Keep it dumb.** Not the dog's name or a birthday — anything a scammer
  could fish out of a social feed. Something random and boring: a two-word
  phrase you wouldn't publish anywhere.
- **One shared rule:** If someone calls claiming to be family in trouble
  and asks for money or personal info, ask for the passphrase. No
  passphrase, no matter how scared you both are — hang up and call back on
  the number you already have.
- **No shame loop.** Agree in advance that a failed question ("uhh, hold
  on...") is the system *working*, not an insult. That agreement is what
  makes people use it under pressure.

If the caller can't give the passphrase: hang up, and call the person on
the number you saved years ago. If the caller objects ("the police told me
not to hang up!"), that objection is itself the tell.

## The hang-up-and-call-back drill (30 seconds, do it every time)

1. **Hang up.** Say "I'm going to call you right back," and end the call.
   Scammers fear a callback to a known number more than anything else.
2. **Call the person yourself**, on your own contact list — or their
   workplace, school, or a sibling. If they answer in a normal voice, the
   crisis was fiction (or verification would work if it's real).
3. **If you can't reach them and the "crisis" money request stands,** call
   one more mutual person before moving any money. Two minutes of calls
   beats every voice clone on earth.

> A real emergency is still a real emergency ten minutes later. No genuine
> hospital, police department, or airline needs your decision during the
> same phone call that told you the news.

## Other clone scams to know

- **The boss.** A text or call from "your manager" about an urgent
  purchase, invoice or transfer — same defense: verify through a channel
  you know (chapter 07).
- **The debt agent / threat call.** "This is the county clerk's office, a
  final notice, you'll be arrested today." Real courts do not arrest
  people by phone for skipping jury duty or a bill.
- **The sweet voice that wants to *help you make money.*** A friendly
  unknown caller with a "sure-fire investment" is usually the opening of a
  long-kindness scam aimed at your savings; see [the stories](stories.md).

## Print this line for the fridge

**Love the voice, hang up anyway.** Call the number you know. One shared
passphrase. No money until two real people agree.

The same principle — verify on your own channel, never inside theirs — is
what defends you from fake *websites* too, which is next.
